Times are for a class of thirty.

Four ways to mark. The fastest is the one nobody guesses.

Most teachers settle on two of them — one for quizzes, one for real tests on paper.

~2min

Google Form or spreadsheet

Quizzes, and anything students type. Nothing to scan.

Import the responses CSV and tap the right option for each multiple-choice question. Those are then marked on the phone for free, and every student's answer to one written question is marked in a single pass, so the class is judged the same way.

~6min

Print it stamped, then scan the stack

Real tests on paper. The only route that cannot mix students up.

Markless prints every copy with its own code in the footer of every page. Feed the collected stack through the copier as one PDF and pages sharing a code reassemble — shuffled, creased or out of order.

~6min

Scan a stack you already printed

A test printed and written on the ordinary way.

One scanned PDF splits back into papers on the device. You see the split before anything is marked, and telling it how many students to expect catches a page the feeder swallowed.

~15min

Photograph each paper

A few papers, a late submission, or no copier in reach.

Prop the phone up and slide each page underneath. It shoots on its own and tells you with a sound or a buzz when the page has landed and it is time for the next one.

The first paper of a set is always marked on its own and shown to you before the other twenty-nine go ahead. A wrong answer key costs one paper, never thirty — on every plan, including free.

You can change any of it.

Real marking, not a vibe check

Three things hold at the edges, where marking software usually stops being honest.

  • Marks you could argue with

    Question-by-question scores, half and quarter marks, and a written justification for every deduction — then feedback that names what to do differently instead of praising effort.

  • It refuses to guess

    A hand-drawn diagram, an unreadable page, a right answer reached an unexpected way: it says so and hands that question back to you instead of inventing a score.

  • You decide the grade. Always.

    Change any mark and the override is recorded. Markless sends nothing to a gradebook, a student or a parent — you carry the mark forward yourself.

Also in the app: answer keys, scanned once or learned from the first paper of a stack · Ontario KTCA categories and curriculum expectations for your region · gradebook CSV and Google Drive export · drafted quizzes, worksheets and lesson plans · and your work following your account onto a new phone. How all of it works, in detail.

Including the parts that are not perfect.

What leaves the phone, stated plainly

You would be handing us other people's children's work. Here is exactly what happens to it.

  • The name is blacked out before upload

    On iOS and Android, on-device text recognition finds identity fields — Name:, Student:, Student ID: — and paints them solid black in the uploaded copy. The unaltered page stays on your phone, and the name files the result under the right student.

  • Imported answers go up keyed by row number

    Never by name. The name column is never transmitted, known student names are scrubbed out of the answer text, and multiple-choice questions never leave the device at all.

  • Pages are not kept, and nothing is tracked

    Images are marked and discarded; what is stored is the result. No ads, no analytics SDK, no student accounts. Delete your account in the app and every result, class and key goes with it, irreversibly.

Where the promise stops

  • Redaction covers name fields. A name inside an essay, a signature on artwork, or handwriting the recognizer cannot read gets through — and the app reports when that happened.
  • It does not run in a browser at all: the web version has no on-device recognition, so photographed work is sent exactly as picked, name included. Use the app, or the Form and CSV route, which is unaffected.
  • One cheap route for objective and short answers goes to a provider whose terms permit training on submitted content and store data in the People's Republic of China. Answer text only — never images, never names — and it can be switched off.
  • No SOC 2 report, no third-party security audit, and no signed DPA template yet; one is available on request.

Markless reduces exposure substantially. It does not make a scanned page anonymous, and no vendor should claim otherwise. Subprocessors, legal footing, retention and every known gap are in Security and compliance.
